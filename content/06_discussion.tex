\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Evidence Stability Under App-Update Churn}

The fixed recent-evidence window separates publication readiness from the continuously changing app-store queue. Once a fully identified build is linked to qualifying static and runtime evidence, a later store update does not invalidate that observation; the newer build becomes a candidate for future longitudinal comparison. Package version, APK hash, static run, dynamic run, and QA provenance keep each claim locked to a recent, reconstructable build rather than to whatever version happens to be installed at writing time.

That locking supports provenance, traceability, and computational reconstruction of the reported rows. It does not establish temporal repeatability. Multiple captures inside one 14-day window characterize within-window behavior for the selected builds; they are not independent collection periods. Measuring longitudinal behavioral drift would require another evidence window under the same identity contract.

\subsection{Interpreting Static and Runtime Risk Posture}

Static analysis characterizes potential packaged exposure: permissions, exported surfaces, configuration choices, and detector findings. Runtime analysis characterizes observed communication behavior during selected strict-idle, QFG, and interactive scenarios, including packet intensity and retained DNS/SNI hostname breadth. Neither construct is equivalent to realized harm. A high finding count does not prove exploitation, and limited observed activity does not demonstrate low exposure.

The four cells of Table~\ref{tab:integrated-profile-matrix} are therefore more defensible than a scalar risk score. High-static/high-runtime is a review priority across both measured layers. High-static/lower-runtime indicates packaged exposure that was not strongly exercised as retained-hostname activity in the sampled scenario. Lower-static/higher-runtime indicates observed communication that detector volume alone does not capture. Lower-static/lower-runtime records lower values for the selected indicators relative to this cohort, not proof of safety. Disagreement between the layers is risk-relevant information, not a contradiction to be averaged away.

The near-zero association between finding volume and interactive retained-host count persists after excluding Snapchat (Table~\ref{tab:statistical-summary}), so the cohort-level result is not solely an artifact of the largest static outlier. With $n=15$, that robustness check does not prove independence. Third-party Disconnect matches add privacy-relevant infrastructure context for retained hostnames after excluding each app's own registrable domain; news-app concentration in that count is not a category-wide risk ranking. A blocklist association does not reveal encrypted payloads or prove leakage.

\subsection{QFG Separation and Practical Implications}

A foregrounded but untouched app may continue fetching content, synchronizing services, or maintaining communication state. Merging QFG into strict idle inflates the quiet baseline; discarding QFG loses meaningful autonomous app activity. The 23 QFG captures are therefore retained as a separate class.

Strict-idle remains the preferred baseline when it is available. QFG is reported separately and is used as the baseline $B$ only where qualifying strict-idle evidence is absent; in this cohort that fallback applies only to TikTok. Future runtime studies should distinguish no-touch foreground behavior from strict idle rather than collapsing all noninteractive execution.
