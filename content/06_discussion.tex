\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Evidence Stability Under App-Update Churn}

When a selected app build is captured within the 14-day window and linked to valid static and runtime evidence, a later app-store update does not invalidate those observations; the newer build instead becomes a candidate for future longitudinal comparison. Each claim remains tied to package version, APK hash, static run, dynamic run, PCAP-derived features, and QA provenance.

\subsection{Interpreting Static and Runtime Evidence}

Static and runtime evidence describe related but different dimensions of Android app risk posture. Static analysis characterizes potential exposure: declared capabilities, packaged attack surface, unguarded-export findings, permissions, configuration choices, and detector rows. Runtime analysis characterizes observed behavior during selected strict-idle, QFG, and interactive scenarios, including communication intensity and retained DNS/SNI hostname breadth. In this dataset, the selected evidence contains 123 dynamic runs across 15 apps, including 44 strict-idle, 23 QFG, and 56 interactive captures. The median additive-smoothed packet-rate shift is 3.39 on the $\log_2$ scale. A high static finding count therefore does not necessarily imply broad retained-hostname activity, while limited observed runtime activity does not demonstrate low privacy or security exposure. Their disagreement is risk-relevant: low observed activity does not negate a broad packaged surface, and high runtime activity does not by itself establish exploitation. The integrated results should be interpreted as cross-layer characterization rather than evidence of causation, prediction, exploitability, or malicious behavior.

\subsection{App-Level Profiles and Review Prioritization}

The app-level profiles illustrate concrete triage implications rather than a ranking. Static-heavy apps such as Snapchat and WhatsApp expose substantial packaged surfaces even when selected runtime scenarios do not produce the broadest hostname breadth. Snapchat is the clearest static outlier in Fig.~\ref{fig:static-runtime-scatter}; finding volume here is exposure volume, not severity-weighted realized risk. WhatsApp combines a high baseline-relative packet-rate shift with messaging-oriented runtime behavior. Signal and Telegram also show large packet-rate shifts from quiet baselines, but their observed hostname breadth remains narrow under the selected scenarios. Lower-static but higher-runtime cases such as CNN and BBC News suggest follow-up scenario and service-family review, while Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, and X (Twitter) exhibit higher values across both layers and may warrant deeper manual examination. These profiles are not composite scores.

\subsection{QFG Separation and Practical Implications}

Separation of QFG captures from strict-idle captures is an important methodological distinction. Social, news, and messaging apps may continue loading content, synchronizing services, or maintaining communication state while foregrounded but untouched. In this study, 23 selected QFG captures are retained as a separate evidence class rather than being merged into strict idle or discarded. Counting this activity as strict-idle would overstate baseline quietness, whereas discarding it would remove meaningful app-driven behavior. Keeping QFG separate allows the paper to describe app-driven foreground behavior without claiming that it represents user inactivity in the strictest sense.
