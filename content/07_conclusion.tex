\section{Conclusion}

This study presented a build-aligned static-runtime evidence model for evaluating the privacy and security risk posture of 15 Android consumer apps. A fixed 14-day window served as a provenance and build-alignment control rather than a longitudinal experiment. The workflow preserved package and version identity, APK hashes, selected static and dynamic run identifiers, and quality-assurance provenance, ensuring that reported observations remained tied to specific app builds despite app-store churn. At cutoff, all 15 apps had usable build-backed evidence spanning both static and runtime layers.

Static analysis identified 1,235 high- and medium-severity findings and 955 unguarded-export findings. Runtime analysis examined 123 captures (44 strict-idle, 23 QFG, and 56 interactive), with a median additive-smoothed $\log_{2}$ packet-rate shift of 3.39. The association between static finding volume and interactive retained-host count was near zero (Table~\ref{tab:statistical-summary}), and this near-zero relationship persisted after excluding Snapchat. These results reinforce that packaged exposure and observed runtime behavior capture distinct, complementary dimensions of app risk posture rather than interchangeable measures.

The combined evidence provides an auditable characterization of privacy and security posture while preserving the distinction between potential exposure and observed behavior; neither layer should be interpreted as proof of exploitability, maliciousness, private-data leakage, or a calibrated probability of harm. Tracker matching adds privacy-relevant infrastructure context within the study's metadata-only boundary, but does not establish tracking behavior or payload semantics. Future work will extend the same provenance contract to longitudinal build-to-build comparisons, additional devices and Android versions, and successive evidence windows for evaluating behavioral change over time.
