\section{Conclusion}

This study presented a static running time evidence model aligned to the build to assess the privacy and security risk posture of 15 consumer apps on Android. A fixed 14-day window served as a provenance and build-alignment control rather than a longitudinal experiment, while package/version identity, APK hashes, run identifiers, and quality-assurance provenance kept observations tied to specific builds despite app-store churn. Static analysis identified 1,235 high- and medium-severity findings and 955 unguarded-export findings, while runtime analysis examined 123 captures (44 strict-idle, 23 QFG, and 56 interactive) with a median additive-smoothed $\log_{2}$ packet-rate shift of 3.39. The association between static finding volume and interactive retained-host count was near zero (Table~\ref{tab:statistical-summary}) and remained near zero after excluding Snapchat.

Together, the results show that packaged exposure and observed runtime behavior provide distinct, complementary dimensions of app risk posture rather than interchangeable measures. Neither layer should be interpreted as proof of exploitability, maliciousness, private-data leakage, or a calibrated probability of harm; tracker matching provides only privacy-relevant infrastructure context within the metadata-only boundary. Future work will extend the same provenance contract to longitudinal build comparisons, additional devices and Android versions, and successive evidence windows for evaluating behavioral change over time.
