\section{Conclusion}

This study presented a build-aligned static-runtime evidence model for evaluating the privacy and security risk posture of 15 Android consumer apps. A fixed 14-day window served as a provenance and build-alignment control rather than a longitudinal experiment. At cutoff, all 15 apps had usable build-backed evidence.

Static analysis identified 1,235 high- and medium-severity findings and 955 unguarded-export findings. Runtime analysis examined 123 captures (44 strict-idle, 23 QFG, and 56 interactive), with a median additive-smoothed $\log_{2}$ packet-rate shift of 3.39. The association between static finding volume and interactive retained-host count was near zero (Table~\ref{tab:statistical-summary}).

The two layers provide an auditable characterization of risk posture while preserving the distinction between potential exposure and observed behavior; neither should be read as exploitability, malice, or a calibrated probability of harm. Future work includes longitudinal build-to-build comparisons, additional devices and Android versions, and successive evidence windows under the same provenance contract.
