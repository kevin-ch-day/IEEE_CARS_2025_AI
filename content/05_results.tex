\section{Results}

\subsection{Dataset and Evidence Provenance}

The final evidence bundle included all 15 selected builds with qualifying static evidence and both baseline-side and interactive-side runtime evidence, although evidence-class depth varied by app. Table~\ref{tab:cutoff-evidence-summary} reports the selected runtime evidence by app and evidence class: 123 captures comprising 44 strict-idle, 23 QFG, and 56 interactive runs. Every app contributes interactive evidence and a baseline-side class, but the baseline is not uniform. Fourteen apps contribute strict-idle captures; TikTok contributes QFG only. Nine apps contribute QFG captures, so the class is not a single-app artifact. Interactive depth ranges from one to seven selected runs, which later motivates reporting within-window packet-rate dispersion only for apps with at least three interactive captures.

Counts represent build-aligned runs selected from the 14-day evidence window. Full app versions, APK hashes, static and dynamic run identifiers, and alignment records are retained in the generated source bundle. Results therefore refer to selected package/version groups rather than mutable installed-app state: a later store update does not revise these rows.

\subsection{Static Exposure Across Selected Builds}

Static exposure varied substantially across the selected builds. Fig.~\ref{fig:static-exposure} compares high/medium findings, unguarded-export findings, and dangerous permissions. High/medium findings ranged from 16 (BBC News, Pinterest) to 325 (Snapchat), with Facebook (164) and Instagram (128) also in the upper tail; unguarded-export findings followed the same concentration (Snapchat 308, Facebook 118, Instagram 91). Dangerous-permission counts were less dispersed, typically 18--20 for several messaging and social apps.

Across all selected builds, the static export contained 1,235 high/medium findings and 955 unguarded-export findings. The median app had 52 high/medium findings and 45 unguarded-export findings, so the largest apps dominate the upper tail. Platform-aligned findings closely track the high/medium totals, while network-security findings are much smaller, ranging from 0 to 6 per app. These values are descriptive exposure counts, not calibrated exploitability or compliance scores.

\subsection{Runtime Coverage and Behavioral Variation}

Table~\ref{tab:runtime-behavior-summary} and Fig.~\ref{fig:runtime-coverage} report the selected runtime evidence: 123 dynamic runs (44 strict-idle, 23 QFG, and 56 interactive). QFG is retained separately because treating that activity as strict-idle would overstate baseline quietness; a foregrounded but untouched app may continue loading content, synchronizing services, or maintaining communication state. Every app contributes at least five selected runs, with the deepest coverage on CNN (12) and BBC News (10). Interaction produced clearer changes in packet rate and traffic volume than in retained hostname count. TikTok, Instagram, WhatsApp, and Facebook exhibited some of the largest interactive packet-rate or traffic-volume measurements.

Interactive packet rates ranged from 14.0 packets/s (The Guardian) to 285.7 packets/s (TikTok), with a median of 41.6 packets/s. Interactive median transfer volume ranged from about 10.1\,MB (Facebook Messenger) to 211.3\,MB (Instagram). Hostname breadth was less dispersed: the median interactive retained-host count was 12, and most content apps clustered between 10 and 16 hosts. Messaging apps need a different reading: WhatsApp, Signal, and Telegram show large baseline-relative packet-rate shifts but three or fewer interactive hosts. A zero retained-hostname value does not imply an absence of traffic; packet activity may occur without a retained DNS/SNI name. Distinct third-party tracker-associated retained hostnames across the selected interactive runs were concentrated in the news apps---BBC News (15), The Guardian (13), and CNN (11)---while several social and messaging apps had none under this metric in the selected scenarios.

\subsection{Integrated Static--Runtime Findings}

Table~\ref{tab:integrated-profile-matrix} summarizes four cohort-relative profiles, not absolute risk ratings: lower on both, runtime-broad, static-heavy, and higher on both. The median cutoffs are 52 high/medium findings and 12 interactive retained hostnames, with ties assigned to the higher group. The resulting 3/4/4/4 occupancy shows that packaged exposure and retained-hostname activity can be jointly elevated, discordant, or jointly lower within this cohort. The runtime-broad and static-heavy profiles are equally populated, so disagreement is not a rare exception. The lower-on-both profile contains three apps and is not proof of safety; both cutoffs are internal to this 15-app set.

Fig.~\ref{fig:static-runtime-hosts} plots static exposure, $\log_{10}(1+\text{high/medium findings})$, against interactive retained-host breadth. The median reference lines reproduce the cohort cutoffs used in Table~IV, and retained-host breadth is the runtime measure used in the Spearman findings-vs-hosts analysis. Similar static finding volumes can therefore correspond to substantially different communication breadth.

\subsection{App-Level Divergence and Representative Cases}

Snapchat is the clearest static-heavy case: its interactive retained-host median (11) sits just below the cohort cutoff of 12. Facebook, TikTok, and Instagram are elevated on both layers, while CNN and BBC News are lower-static but runtime-broad. Signal, Telegram, and WhatsApp show large packet-rate shifts from quiet baselines with narrow hostname breadth. Fig.~\ref{fig:static-runtime-pps} provides the complementary runtime intensity view as the additive-smoothed packet-rate shift $\log_2[(I+1)/(B+1)]$, using the strict-idle median as $B$ when available and QFG otherwise. Hostname breadth and packet-rate shift capture different runtime properties; narrow breadth can coexist with a large PPS shift. Table~\ref{tab:statistical-summary} summarizes the association between the findings and the hosts, which remains near zero after excluding Snapchat.

Among the 11 apps with at least three selected interactive captures, the median IQR of the packet-rate within the app was 63.5 packets/s (range: 7.5--282.8 packets / s). This dispersion within the window is not a measure of longitudinal repeatability.
