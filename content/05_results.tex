\subsection{Dataset and Evidence Provenance}

The final evidence bundle included all 15 selected builds with qualifying static evidence and both baseline-side and interactive-side runtime evidence, although evidence-class depth varied by app. Table~\ref{tab:cutoff-evidence-summary} reports the selected runtime evidence by app and evidence class. Counts represent build-aligned runs selected from the 14-day evidence window. Full app versions, APK hashes, static and dynamic run identifiers, and alignment records are retained in the generated source bundle. Results therefore refer to selected package/version groups rather than mutable installed-app state.

\subsection{Static Exposure Across Selected Builds}

Static exposure varied substantially across the selected builds. Fig.~\ref{fig:static-exposure} compares high/medium findings, unguarded-export findings, and dangerous permissions. High/medium findings ranged from 16 (BBC News, Pinterest) to 325 (Snapchat), with Facebook (164) and Instagram (128) also in the upper tail; unguarded-export findings followed the same concentration (Snapchat 308, Facebook 118, Instagram 91). Dangerous-permission counts were less dispersed, typically 18--20 for several messaging and social apps.

Across all selected builds, the static export contained 1,235 high/medium findings and 955 unguarded-export findings. The median app had 52 high/medium findings and 45 unguarded-export findings, so the largest apps dominate the upper tail. Platform-aligned findings closely track the high/medium totals, while network-security findings are much smaller, ranging from 0 to 6 per app. These values are descriptive exposure counts, not calibrated exploitability or compliance scores.

\subsection{Runtime Coverage and Behavioral Variation}

Table~\ref{tab:runtime-behavior-summary} and Fig.~\ref{fig:runtime-coverage} report the selected runtime evidence: 123 dynamic runs (44 strict-idle, 23 QFG, and 56 interactive). QFG is retained separately because a foregrounded but untouched app may continue loading content, synchronizing services, or maintaining communication state; treating that activity as strict-idle would overstate baseline quietness. Every app contributes at least five selected runs, with the deepest coverage on CNN (12) and BBC News (10). Interaction produced clearer changes in packet rate and traffic volume than in retained hostname count. TikTok, Instagram, WhatsApp, and Facebook exhibited some of the largest interactive packet-rate or traffic-volume measurements.

Interactive packet rates ranged from 14.0 packets/s (The Guardian) to 285.7 packets/s (TikTok), with a median of 41.6 packets/s. Interactive median transfer volume ranged from about 10.1\,MB (Facebook Messenger) to 211.3\,MB (Instagram). Hostname breadth was less dispersed: the median interactive retained-host count was 12, and most content apps clustered between 10 and 16 hosts. Messaging apps need a different reading: WhatsApp, Signal, and Telegram show large baseline-relative packet-rate shifts but three or fewer interactive hosts. A zero retained-hostname value does not imply an absence of traffic; packet activity may occur without a retained DNS/SNI name. Third-party Disconnect matches were concentrated in the news apps---BBC News (15), The Guardian (13), and CNN (11)---while several social and messaging apps had no such matches in the selected scenarios.

\subsection{Integrated Static--Runtime Findings}

Table~\ref{tab:integrated-profile-matrix} is a cohort-relative risk-posture map, not an absolute risk rating. Apps at or above both median splits occupy the high/high cell and are a cross-layer review priority because packaged exposure and observed hostname breadth are jointly elevated. High-static/lower-runtime indicates packaged exposure that was not broadly expressed through retained hostname activity in the selected scenarios. Lower-static/higher-runtime indicates broader observed communication despite lower detector volume. Lower/lower records comparatively lower measured values within this cohort only; it is not proof of safety. The 4/4/4/3 occupancy shows that all four profile types are represented. The near-zero rank association in Table~\ref{tab:statistical-summary} therefore supports retaining static exposure and runtime behavior as separate dimensions rather than forcing a composite score.

\subsection{App-Level Divergence and Representative Cases}

Snapchat is the clearest static-heavy case: its interactive retained-host median (11) sits just below the cohort cutoff of 12. Facebook, TikTok, and Instagram are elevated on both layers, while CNN and BBC News are lower-static but runtime-broad. Signal, Telegram, and WhatsApp show large packet-rate shifts from quiet baselines with narrow hostname breadth. Fig.~\ref{fig:static-runtime-scatter} and Table~\ref{tab:statistical-summary} show the same pattern. The near-zero association persists after excluding Snapchat, so a static-only table would miss runtime-heavy apps and a runtime-only table would miss packaged exposure.
