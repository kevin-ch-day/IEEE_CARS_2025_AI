\section{Results}

\subsection{Dataset and Evidence Provenance}

The final evidence bundle included all 15 selected builds with qualifying static evidence and both baseline-side and interactive-side runtime evidence, although evidence-class depth varied by app. Table~\ref{tab:cutoff-evidence-summary} reports the runtime evidence selected by app and evidence class: 123 captures comprising 44 strict-idle, 23 QFG, and 56 interactive runs. Every app contributes interactive evidence and a baseline-side class, but the baseline is not uniform. Fourteen apps contribute strict-idle captures; TikTok contributes QFG only. Nine apps contribute QFG captures, so the class is not a single-app artifact. The interactive depth ranges from one to seven selected runs, which later motivates reporting within-window packet-rate dispersion only for apps with at least three interactive captures.

Counts represent build-aligned runs selected from the 14-day evidence window. Full app versions, APK hashes, static and dynamic run identifiers, and alignment records are retained in the generated source bundle. Results therefore refer to selected package/version groups rather than mutable installed-app state: a later store update does not revise these rows.

\subsection{Static Exposure Across Selected Builds}

Static exposure varied substantially among the selected builds. Fig.~\ref{fig:static-exposure} compares high/medium findings, unprotected export findings, and dangerous permissions. The High/medium findings ranged from 16 (BBC News, Pinterest) to 325 (Snapchat), with Facebook (164) and Instagram (128) also in the upper tail; the unguarded-export findings followed the same concentration (Snapchat 308, Facebook 118, Instagram 91). Dangerous-permission counts were less dispersed, typically 18--20 for several messaging and social apps.

Across all selected builds, the static export contained 1,235 high/medium findings and 955 unguarded-export findings. The median app had 52 high/medium findings and 45 unguarded-export findings, so the largest apps dominate the upper tail. The Platform-aligned findings closely track the high/medium totals, while the network-security findings are much smaller, ranging from 0 to 6 per app. These values are descriptive exposure counts, not calibrated exploitability or compliance scores.

\subsection{Runtime Coverage and Behavioral Variation}

Table~\ref{tab:runtime-behavior-summary} and Fig.~\ref{fig:runtime-coverage} report the selected runtime evidence: 123 dynamic runs (44 strict-idle, 23 QFG, and 56 interactive). QFG is retained separately because a foregrounded but untouched app may continue loading content, synchronizing services, or maintaining communication state; treating that activity as strict-idle would overstate baseline quietness. Each app contributes at least five selected runs, with the deepest coverage on CNN (12) and BBC News (10). The interaction produced clearer changes in packet rate and traffic volume than in the retained hostname count. TikTok, Instagram, WhatsApp, and Facebook exhibited some of the largest interactive packet-rate or traffic-volume measurements.

The interactive packet rates ranged from 14.0 packets/s (The Guardian) to 285.7 packets / s (TikTok), with a median of 41.6 packets / s. The median interactive transfer volume ranged from about 10.1 \, MB (Facebook Messenger) to 211.3 \, MB (Instagram). Hostname breadth was less dispersed: the median interactive retained-host count was 12, and most content apps clustered between 10 and 16 hosts. Messaging apps need a different reading: WhatsApp, Signal, and Telegram show large baseline-relative packet-rate shifts but three or fewer interactive hosts. A zero retained-hostname value does not imply an absence of traffic; packet activity may occur without a retained DNS/SNI name. Different retained hostnames associated with third-party trackers in the selected interactive runs were concentrated in the news apps --BBC News (15), The Guardian (13), and CNN (11)---while several social and messaging apps had none under this metric in the selected scenarios.

\subsection{Integrated Static--Runtime Findings}

Table~\ref{tab:integrated-profile-matrix} is a cohort-relative risk-posture map, not an absolute risk rating. Higher static exposure is high- and medium-severity finding volume at or above the cohort median (52); higher runtime activity is interactive retained-hostname count at or above the cohort median (12); ties are assigned to the higher group. The resulting 4/4/4/3 occupancy shows that packaged exposure and retained-hostname activity can be jointly elevated, discordant, or jointly lower within this cohort. The two discordant cells are equally populated, so disagreement is not a rare exception: an app may present substantial packaged exposure with comparatively narrow interactive hostname breadth or the reverse. The lower/lower cell contains three apps and is not a proof of safety; both cutoffs are internal to this set of 15-apps.

The association of near-zero rank in Table~\ref{tab:statistical-summary} therefore supports retaining static exposure and runtime behavior as separate dimensions rather than forcing a composite score. Fig.~\ref{fig:static-runtime-scatter} reports packet-rate shift as a complementary runtime intensity measure; it is not the axis used to assign the Table~\ref{tab:integrated-profile-matrix} cells.

\subsection{App-Level Divergence and Representative Cases}

Snapchat is the clearest static-heavy case: its interactive retained-host median (11) sits just below the cohort cutoff of 12. Facebook, TikTok, and Instagram are elevated on both layers, while CNN and BBC News are lower-static but runtime-broad. Signal, Telegram, and WhatsApp show large packet-rate shifts from quiet baselines with narrow hostname breadth. Fig.~\ref{fig:static-runtime-scatter} and Table~\ref{tab:statistical-summary} summarize this pattern. The near-zero association persists after excluding Snapchat. Among the 11 apps with at least three selected interactive captures, the median within-app packet-rate IQR was 63.5 packets/s, with app-level IQRs ranging from 7.5 to 282.8 packets/s. This dispersion characterizes repeated observations within the fixed evidence window and is not a measure of longitudinal repeatability.
